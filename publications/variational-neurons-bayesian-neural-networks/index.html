Variational Neurons and Bayesian Neural Networks: Distinct Probabilistic Objects, Inference Loci, and Computational Granularity

Yves Ruffenach · Conservatoire National des Arts et Métiers, Strasbourg, France · ORCID 0009-0009-4737-0555

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21870089

Read PDF ↗View on Zenodo ↗

Abstract

Probabilistic neural architectures frequently reuse the same mathematical devices—priors, Kullback–Leibler (KL) terms, reparameterized samples, Monte Carlo evaluation, and predictive distributions—while assigning them to different random objects and computational loci. This article provides a clarifying taxonomy for these constructions and formalizes an operational notion of the variational neuron. Four non-equivalent axes are separated: Bayesianity, variationality, stochasticity, and distributional computation. Six reporting questions then identify the random object, inferential or regularization locus, conditioning information, sampling location, propagated representation, and prediction rule. A standard Bayesian neural network (BNN) represents dataset-conditioned uncertainty over parameters or functions and predicts by posterior marginalization. By contrast, the Elemental Variational Expanse (EVE) denotes a unit-level regime in which an input-conditioned latent distribution is placed inside a declared hidden computational unit, contributes a separately measurable variational term, participates explicitly in the forward map, and exposes unit-indexed diagnostics. The framework distinguishes a strict variational latent neuron, whose distribution approximates a posterior under an explicit latent-variable model, from a variationally regularized distributional neuron trained end-to-end without an independent local likelihood. It also positions variational neurons relative to major neighboring probabilistic architectures. A reproducible five-seed heteroscedastic benchmark serves as a controlled illustration: EVE attains lower mean negative log-likelihood (NLL) and continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) than a capacity-matched deterministic cell at similar mean squared error (MSE), while its narrower intervals accompany slightly lower coverage. These results demonstrate distinct probabilistic utility arising from distributional computation at the hidden-unit level.
